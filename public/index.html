Why a bank should care

Business clients leave before they can bank digitally.

70%

In August 2025, Fenergo surveyed 600 senior decision-makers at banks and financial firms. 70% had lost clients in the past year because onboarding took too long.

That share has climbed from 48% in 2023 and 67% in 2024. About 1 in 10 onboarding applications is now abandoned, and commercial and corporate banking carries the heaviest load.

Fenergo, 2025 industry survey
Companies with joint signatories wait the longest.

A member of a customer fulfilment operations team told me one setup takes 4 to 5 days, because every partner has to be checked, and the company can't move money until all of it is done.

My interview
They're worth far more than one account.

McKinsey found that more than 1 in 5 dollars banks earn comes from small and mid-sized businesses. That money doesn't come from one fee. It comes from the deposits, payments, payroll, trade and FX a business runs through its bank every month it stays.

McKinsey
In the UAE, they're most of the economy.

SMEs make up more than 95% of UAE companies and employ about 86% of private-sector workers, according to the UAE Central Bank. Every bank wants them, so the bank that gets them banking fastest wins them.

UAE Central Bank, via Arab News

So every day taken out of setup keeps a client the bank has already won.

The work isn't hard. It's slow.

Most of the 4 to 5 days go on one loop: the maker sets up access, and the checker finds a mismatch and sends it back, four times over. With Jointly, the agent takes the maker's seat and the checker still decides.

Why an agent

Why not just a better digital form?

A better form doesn't remove the work to get digital banking access. It moves it onto the customer.

The obvious fix is a self-serve form in the business banking app. I designed one at a UAE bank. It replaced the paper form the partners signed together and the passwords sent by courier, but the business owners, the bank's customers, ended up doing most of the work.

Both screens are illustrative, not any bank's actual app.

What the form fixes

The partners stop signing a paper form, and passwords stop travelling by courier.

What it costs

The partners do the bank's work instead, and everyone waits for the slowest one. Many give up: in one survey, 87% of corporate treasurers said they had abandoned a bank application.

Retail Banker International
What the agent does

It reads what the bank already has. A human checker approves once, and each partner can start banking straight away.

    The skill besides product design

    Most people think the hard part of building an agent is the AI. It isn't.

    It's service design.

    Service design maps how the work moves between the customer, the team and the bank's systems. That's the work an agent takes over, and I learnt it at a UAE bank.

    The things people ask before they try it.

    Something I missed? Write to me, I answer.

    01Is any of this real bank data?

    No. Every name, number and document is synthetic, and the systems are modelled on common core-banking setups.

    02What does the human checker still decide?

    They review the workflow setup the agent built and approve it once. If two records disagree, like two different signing limits, the agent asks them which one is right.

    03What if the agent gets something wrong?

    It can't change anything before the human checker approves. After it sets up access, it reads every change back, and any difference stops the case.

    04Does it work with the bank's legacy systems?

    Yes. It reads the systems the bank already has and writes to one place, so nothing needs replacing.

    05Who designed and built it?

    I did the research, service design and product design. The prototype was built with Devin from my prompts.
